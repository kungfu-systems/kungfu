# KF-ADR-019fad41-07fe-7f1e-a37a-a2572357700c: Govern every deprecation through one release lifecycle

- Status: accepted; implemented by PR #1879
- Date: 2026-07-29
- Category: compatibility / release governance / retained history
- Preserves: KFD-1 versioning, stable compatibility promises, KFX
  contribution boundaries, ADR delivery, Warrant authority, and historical
  evidence

## Context

Kungfu has accumulated correct but local removal mechanisms: Core architecture
carried a small deprecation ledger, CLI cleanup used an alias-specific gate,
release design described compatibility boundaries, and individual ADRs retained
their own migration evidence. These mechanisms did not answer one repository
question: for every deprecated surface, is removal not due, due, already
qualified, or covered by one exact and bounded exception?

The missing common lifecycle creates two opposite risks. A stale compatibility
path can survive indefinitely because no release owns its settlement, while a
current surface can disappear because a local absence check is mistaken for
permission to remove it. Stable CLI, SDK, public API, KFX, artifact, document,
and persisted protocol promises need different boundaries, but they must be
evaluated by one deterministic policy.

## Decision

Kungfu adopts
`developer/deprecation/deprecation-lifecycle.contract.json` as the common
lifecycle contract and
`developer/deprecation/deprecation-registry.json` as its source registry.
Subsystem ledgers may contribute entries and evidence; they may not own a
different lifecycle, due calculation, Warrant interpretation, or release
settlement rule.

The companion
`developer/deprecation/deprecation-discovery.contract.json` defines the
machine-recognizable enrollment boundary. Every live C++, Python, JavaScript
or TypeScript, persisted schema or protocol, structured CLI, KFX, artifact, or
document deprecation binds one common registry entry plus one marker identity.
The registry declares that marker's dialect and exact current path. Orphans,
duplicates, path drift, unknown dialects, and live entries without a surface
fail source acceptance.

Generated-code and historical-evidence classifications are explicit contract
records, not incidental search exclusions. They are restricted to exact files
or narrow generated subtrees and retain a reviewable reason. Discovery is a
completeness check only: it cannot accelerate a removal window, settle an
entry, or mint a Warrant.

Each governed entry carries identity, surface class, owner, exact current
surface, replacement, migration guidance, deprecation date and product
version, known consumers, calendar and qualified-release windows, earliest
removal boundary, removal conditions, retained evidence, and an executable
zero-reference audit. Entries author only `active`, `deprecated`, `removed`,
or `settled`. `removal-due` is derived; it is never hand-written.

The surface taxonomy is:

1. internal or experimental;
2. public alpha or preview;
3. stable CLI, SDK, or public API;
4. KFX contribution;
5. document;
6. artifact; and
7. persisted schema or wire protocol.

Defaults are minima, not permissions to weaken a stronger promise. Internal
surfaces may become eligible at any qualified release. Public pre-stable and
document surfaces default to 30 calendar days and one qualified release.
Stable API, KFX, and artifact surfaces default to 90 days and one qualified
release, with removal no earlier than a new major. Persisted schemas and wire
protocols default to 180 days and two qualified releases, a new major, and a
qualified historical reader, export, or migration path.

The **next eligible release** is the first qualified candidate that satisfies
all three conditions:

1. its release date is on or after the deprecation date plus the minimum
   calendar window;
2. the number of qualified releases after the deprecation reaches the declared
   minimum, including the candidate; and
3. its version crosses the surface class's permitted breaking boundary.

Missing release history, ambiguous version syntax, unknown surface class, or
missing protocol support evidence fails closed as `invalid`. A deprecated entry
at its first eligible release is `due`. A protected alpha or stable gate fails
until one of these exact dispositions is present:

- qualified removal with migration, release-note, retained-history, and
  current-head zero-reference evidence;
- restored support with explicit restoration decision and qualification; or
- one exact, bounded, non-renewing `kungfu.warrant` projection.

The registry records only a Warrant projection: authority, exact Warrant Root,
entry identity, authorizer, issue and expiry bounds, last covered release, and
evidence reference. It does not mint, renew, or replace native Warrant
authority. Renewal or implicit rollover fields are invalid.

Removal concerns the current executable surface. Settlement never deletes ADRs,
release notes, migration evidence, historical readers, or audit receipts.
Persisted schema and protocol history remains readable or explicitly
migratable after the current writer moves on.

`./shifu deprecation:audit` is the read-only structured projection.
`./shifu adr:release:gate` evaluates the same authority: development checks its
structure, while protected alpha and stable candidates also fail on applicable
due entries. Promotion rehearsal exercises the same path before publication.

## Consequences

- Every current deprecation has one owner, next action, and release
  disposition.
- A new machine-recognizable marker cannot bypass the common registry by
  leaving lifecycle files untouched.
- Full-tree and changed-file inventory scopes expose both retained drift and
  pull-request-local orphans.
- Class defaults are executable minima, and exact authority plus marker-dialect
  consistency prevents a stable, KFX, artifact, document, schema, or protocol
  promise from being relabeled into a faster class.
- The retained pre-stable CLI 0/0 settlement is one exact-entry-bound
  historical record; it cannot be copied into a new deprecation.
- Calendar time alone and release count alone are insufficient.
- Local contribution ledgers remain useful without becoming competing
  authorities.
- A source search proves current-head absence only; removal permission still
  requires the lifecycle boundary and retained evidence.
- Historical deprecations remain queryable after executable debt reaches zero.
- Warrant exceptions are explicit technical debt with exact, non-renewing
  bounds.

## Replacement criteria

A successor may replace this design only if it preserves one common authority,
the combined calendar/release/boundary calculation, stronger stable and
protocol promises, exact non-renewing Warrant bounds, protected release
admission, current-head zero-reference proof, and immutable historical
evidence.
